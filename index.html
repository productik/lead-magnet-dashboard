Conversions by tool, most to fewest

Visitors = unique people who opened the tool page. Leads = people who gave their details. Registered = a webinar registration on or after using the tool. Bought = a program sale after that. Each tool uses its most complete source (last column): lead records for the four tools that create them, page visits and registration forms for ATS and Mock Interview. Rates are out of leads where there are leads, else out of visitors. Ordered by purchases, then registrations.

Did registrants attend the webinar?

Of the people who registered for a webinar after using each tool, how many actually joined one on Zoom on or after their registration date (matched on HubSpot ID or email), how many stayed 30+ minutes, and whether attendees bought more than people who registered but didn't come. Same order as the ranking above.

How people registered, by tool

Each registration on a tool page, by when it happened: before using the tool, on the results page, or after using the tool but somewhere else. Attended = joined a webinar on Zoom on or after the registration date. Bought afterwards = a finance sale on or after the registration date.

Which button earns the registration

The last registration button pressed before each tracked registration, for every tool with a page.

Registered without ever using the tool

People who registered on a tool page before uploading or starting anything. A large share means the page works as a landing page more than a lead magnet.

Where they registered from

The tool page, the last button pressed before registering, and the webinar they signed up for.

Who they are

From their HubSpot contact (matched on email; Unknown = no match) and their first visit to the tool page.

Do the career tools bring in new people and turn them into webinar registrations and sales? Covers the tools that create lead records: Resume Analyzer, Salary Analyzer, AI Quotient and FDE Gap Analyzer. ATS Resume Checker and AI Mock Interview don't create lead records; see Conversions and Tool funnels for them. Percentages always show the number of people next to them.

How much of IK's sales come after a career tool?

Every IK sale in the period (finance), and whether the buyer had used a career tool before buying. Period filter only. Tool history starts on 18 Apr 2026, so this is a minimum.

What each tool is worth

For each tool: people who used it in the period, how many were new to IK, registered for a webinar, and bought a program afterwards, and the revenue that followed.

Were these people new to IK?

New to IK = their first contact with IK was this tool (their HubSpot contact was created when they used it). Already known = they were in HubSpot before: an earlier lead, webinar or customer. This shows whether the tools find new prospects or mostly re-engage people we already have.

Does trying more tools lead to more sales?

We count how many different career tools each person has used. If people who use two or more tools buy more often, nudging users to try a second tool is worth doing.

Are newer months converting better?

People who first used a tool in each month, and how many registered or bought within 30, 60 and 90 days. Comparing the same time window keeps old and new months fair. A dash means that window hasn't finished yet. Uses Tool, Geo, Source, Device and User, not Period.

How quickly do people register and buy?

Days from first using the tool to the first webinar registration, and to the first purchase.

Who is most likely to register and buy?

People grouped by what they told the tool or where they came from, with how many registered and bought. Uses Period and Tool (other filters are in the local app).

Which lead magnets bring the most registrations and sales, how to judge each one, and who their users are. People = unique emails per tool. Uses the Period filter only; the other filters are in the local app.

Lead magnets ranked by registrations

Each tool's job, the one metric that says whether it works (north star), what moves it, and what must not get worse (guardrails), with today's values.

    Which tools did people use before this one?

    Each row is a tool; each column names an earlier tool and counts the people who used it before their first use of the row's tool. People = first-time users of the row's tool in the period.

    Which tools did registrants use before registering?

    Each row is the tool someone used before registering for a webinar (HubSpot); each column names another tool they had also used before that registration, with the number of people.

    The path to registration: which tool, in what order

    For everyone who registered for a webinar after using a career tool, the tools they used in order (first use of each) up to the day they registered. Read “AI Quotient → Salary Analyzer → Registered” as: used AI Quotient first, then Salary Analyzer, then registered. Covers the four tools with lead records; ATS and Mock Interview can't be placed in a person's history.

    How often each tool is used again, and over what span

    Backend runs per person (one run = one resume within 10 minutes). Same resume only = every repeat used the identical file; different resumes = two or more files. A very high 'most resumes by one person' usually means a bot or an internal tester. Span = days between a repeat user's first and last run.
